What business process automation is

Fewer handoffs. Fewer mistakes. Same team.

Business process automation uses software to carry out the repeatable steps of a workflow, such as moving data, sending notifications, generating documents and routing approvals, so they happen the same way every time without someone doing them by hand.

Rules-based automation handles the predictable steps. AI extends it to the messy ones: reading an emailed invoice, classifying a request, or drafting a reply. We use each where it fits, and keep people in charge of decisions that need judgement.

Good automation starts on paper. Before we build anything, we map the workflow as it actually happens, including the workarounds, because automating a broken process just breaks it faster.

How an engagement works

Small first version.
Evidence before scale.

We would rather automate one workflow that works every day than ten that look good in a demo. Each stage ends with something you can see and decide on.

  1. 01

    Discovery call

    A short conversation about the work as it happens today: who does it, how often, which tools are involved and what a better outcome looks like.

  2. 02

    Map and prioritise

    We map the workflow, mark the steps worth automating and the ones that should stay human, and agree what “working” means in measurable terms.

  3. 03

    Build a focused first version

    One workflow, built around your existing tools, with logging, error handling and a clear way for a person to step in.

  4. 04

    Test with real cases

    We run it against real (or realistic) examples, including the awkward ones, and fix what breaks before anyone depends on it.

  5. 05

    Launch, measure, extend

    It goes live with your team, we watch the results together, and the evidence decides what we automate next.

Frequently asked questions

Questions, answered plainly.

What business processes can be automated?

Processes that are frequent, follow recognisable rules and involve moving information between people or systems: data entry, approvals, notifications, document generation, reporting and onboarding are the usual candidates.

Do you use Zapier, Make or n8n?

We use low-code automation tools when they are reliable and cost-effective for the job, and write custom code when volumes, security or complexity call for it. We explain the trade-off, including running costs, before building.

What if one of our systems has no API?

There are often other options, such as scheduled exports, email parsing or database connections. If no reliable option exists, we’ll say so rather than build something fragile.

How do we know the automation is working?

Every automation we build logs what it does and alerts someone when a step fails. We agree the measures of success, such as hours saved or errors avoided, before we start.

Will automation work with our accounting or ERP software?

Usually, if it offers an API or reliable import and export. We check during discovery.

How much does it cost?

It depends on the number of steps and systems involved. We quote after discovery, and show any subscription costs for automation tools separately.
