Some customers will always prefer to call. For businesses that miss calls during busy hours or after closing, an AI voice agent can answer, help with simple requests and take messages. This article explains how they work and where they fall short.

## What an AI voice agent is

An AI voice agent answers phone calls and talks to callers in natural speech. Behind the scenes it chains three technologies:

1. **Speech recognition** turns the caller’s words into text.
2. **A language model** decides what to say or do, using information you approve and tools you allow, such as checking availability.
3. **Speech synthesis** turns the reply back into a natural-sounding voice.

A **telephony provider** connects all of this to a phone number, so calls to your business line can be routed to the agent.

## What voice agents handle well

- **Frequently asked questions:** opening hours, location of service, prices, what to bring.
- **Appointment requests:** taking the caller’s details and preferred time, and confirming a slot if connected to your calendar.
- **After-hours answering:** taking messages accurately instead of letting calls ring out.
- **Call routing:** working out what the caller needs and transferring them to the right person.
- **Follow-up by text:** sending a confirmation or link by SMS or WhatsApp after the call.

## Where they struggle

- **Noisy lines and accents.** Speech recognition accuracy drops on poor connections and with accents the system wasn’t trained on. Test with real callers.
- **Local languages.** Recognition and synthesis for Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa are improving but less reliable than English.
- **Latency.** Each step adds delay. A pause of more than a second or two feels unnatural.
- **Complex or emotional calls.** Complaints and sensitive conversations should go to a person.

## Considerations for Nigerian businesses

- **Phone numbers.** Not every voice platform offers Nigerian numbers. Call forwarding from your existing line to the platform may be possible, with costs for forwarded minutes. Check before you design.
- **Per-minute costs.** Telephony, speech and model usage are usually billed per minute, often in US dollars. Estimate monthly call volume to understand running costs.
- **Customer preference.** Many Nigerian customers would rather send a WhatsApp message than call. If most of your enquiries arrive in writing, a [WhatsApp assistant](/whatsapp-automation) may deliver more value for less money.
- **Disclosure and consent.** Tell callers they are speaking to an AI assistant, and if calls are recorded or transcribed, say so. The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 applies to call recordings and transcripts.

## Voice or WhatsApp?

Ask three questions:

1. **How do customers contact you now?** Count calls versus messages for two weeks.
2. **How many calls go unanswered?** If very few, voice automation adds little.
3. **What do callers want?** If it is mostly simple information and bookings, an agent can help. If it is mostly complex problem-solving, staff training or better call routing may help more.

Many businesses start with WhatsApp and add voice later, once they know the questions and answers work.

## Designing a voice agent that callers accept

- Introduce it as an AI assistant within the first sentence.
- Keep answers short and confirm key details back to the caller.
- Offer a person, or a call-back, at any point.
- Send a text confirmation after bookings so there is a written record.
- Review call transcripts weekly in the first month and fix what goes wrong.

## Key takeaways

Voice agents are real and useful for answering simple calls, especially after hours. They need careful testing for accents, connection quality and costs, and in Nigeria they often work best alongside WhatsApp rather than instead of it.
