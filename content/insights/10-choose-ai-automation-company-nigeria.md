More and more companies and freelancers in Nigeria offer AI automation. Some are excellent. Others resell a chatbot template with a new logo. This checklist helps you tell the difference before you sign anything. (Yes, we are one of the companies you might compare. Use the same questions on us.)

## Questions about understanding your business

- **Did they ask about your workflow before proposing a tool?** A good provider wants to know how the work happens today, who does it and what a better outcome looks like.
- **Did they suggest starting small?** One workflow, with a clear measure of success, is a better first project than a company-wide “AI transformation”.
- **Did they tell you what not to automate?** Providers who say everything should be automated are selling, not advising.

## Questions about evidence

- **What have they built that you can see?** Live products, demos you can try, or client work they have permission to show.
- **Are their claims labelled honestly?** Look for clear distinctions between client work, their own products and capabilities they offer.
- **Can you speak to a client?** If they have clients, they may be able to introduce you, with permission.

Be wary of large percentage savings or revenue figures with no client name, date or method behind them.

## Questions about technology and data

- **Which platforms will they use?** For WhatsApp automation, insist on Meta’s official WhatsApp Business Platform. Unofficial tools risk your number being banned.
- **Where will your data go, and who can access it?** Ask which AI providers process your customers’ messages and where.
- **How do they handle the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023?** They should talk about data minimisation, access control, retention and telling customers how their data is used. They may not give legal advice, but they should design with the law in mind.
- **Who owns what they build?** Code, configurations, prompts and accounts should be clearly assigned in the contract. Ideally, accounts such as your Meta Business account are in your name.

## Questions about safety and quality

- **How does the system hand over to a person?** There should always be a route to a human.
- **How do they prevent wrong answers?** Look for grounding in your own information, testing with real cases and review of conversations after launch.
- **What can the AI do in your systems?** Permissions should be limited to what the workflow needs, and sensitive actions should require confirmation.
- **How will you know it works?** Agree measures before you start: response times, leads followed up, hours saved.

## Questions about cost

- **What is the build cost, and what is included?**
- **What are the monthly running costs?** WhatsApp message charges from Meta, AI model usage, hosting and any software subscriptions, many of them in US dollars.
- **What does support and maintenance cost after launch?**

Ask for all of this in writing.

## Red flags

- Guaranteed results, guaranteed rankings or guaranteed AI recommendations.
- Bulk WhatsApp messaging to people who haven’t opted in.
- No way for customers to reach a person.
- Pressure to sign before they understand your business.
- Accounts registered in the provider’s name instead of yours.
- Fake reviews or testimonials you can’t verify.

## Local or remote?

A provider doesn’t need an office near you to do good work. Many strong teams, including ours, are remote-first. What matters is clear communication, written agreements and responsiveness. If you need someone on site, for hardware or training, ask about it directly.

## What a good first project looks like

- One workflow, clearly described.
- A written scope with success measures.
- A plan in stages, each one usable.
- Testing with real cases before launch.
- A review after a few weeks of live use, and an honest decision about what comes next.
