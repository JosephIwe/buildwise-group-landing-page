How to Make Your Business More Visible on ChatGPT

What actually affects whether ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews can find and cite your business, based on the providers’ own documentation, and what is myth.

By the Buildwise Group team · 3 November 2026 · 3 min read

More customers now ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Google’s AI Mode which company to use. Business owners understandably want to know how to appear in those answers. There is a lot of confident advice online, and much of it is guesswork. This guide sticks to what the providers have actually published, and to the basics that make a business easy to understand.

How AI search finds businesses

AI assistants with search features don’t just rely on what the model learned during training. When a question needs current information, they search the web, read pages and write an answer, usually citing sources. To be cited, your pages must be findable, readable and useful for the question.

Step 1: Let the right crawlers in

  • ChatGPT search uses a crawler called OAI-SearchBot. OpenAI’s crawler documentation (opens in a new tab) says sites that block it won’t be shown in ChatGPT search answers (though they can still appear as navigational links). OpenAI’s training crawler, GPTBot, is separate: you can allow search and block training, or the reverse.
  • Google’s AI Overviews and AI Mode use Google’s normal search index. Google’s guidance, AI features and your website (opens in a new tab), says there are no additional requirements beyond normal SEO: pages must be indexed and eligible to appear in Search.
  • Perplexity, Bing (which powers parts of several assistants) and others have their own crawlers.

Check your `robots.txt` file, and check your CDN or firewall too. Services such as Cloudflare offer settings that block AI crawlers before they ever read robots.txt. Some businesses are invisible to AI search because of a setting they didn’t know was on.

Step 2: Say clearly who you are and what you do

AI systems need to tell what your business is. On your website:

  • State your company name, what you do, who you serve and where, in plain words near the top of the homepage.
  • Give each main service its own page with a clear heading, so a page can answer one question well.
  • Keep your name, services, location (or service area) and contact details consistent across every page.

Step 3: Answer the questions customers actually ask

Assistants cite pages that answer the question. If customers ask “who builds WhatsApp chatbots in Lagos?”, a page that explains what WhatsApp automation involves, who it suits, what it costs to run and how to start is more citable than a page that says “we are the best”. Write for people first: specific, accurate and useful.

Step 4: Use structured data accurately

Structured data (schema.org markup) helps search engines interpret facts such as your organisation’s name, logo, social profiles and services. Google says structured data should match the visible content of the page. It doesn’t guarantee any appearance, and inaccurate markup can do harm.

Step 5: Build genuine presence beyond your website

AI answers often draw on third-party sources: business directories, review platforms, industry lists, news coverage and community discussions. Accurate profiles on relevant directories, genuine reviews from real clients and mentions in credible publications all help systems understand and trust that your business exists and does what it says.

Never buy fake reviews or create fake “independent” articles about your business. They break platform rules and consumer protection law in many countries, and they are increasingly detected.

Step 6: Measure what you can

  • Google Search Console shows impressions and clicks from Google Search, including AI features, within overall Search totals.
  • Analytics can show visits referred by chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai and similar sites, when they pass a referrer.
  • Ask your customers how they found you, and add “AI assistant” as an option on your enquiry form.
  • Check periodically how AI assistants describe your business, and correct inaccurate information at its source.

Myths to ignore

  • “One file gets you into ChatGPT.” Files such as llms.txt are harmless, but at the time of writing no major AI search provider has said it uses them to decide citations.
  • “Guaranteed AI recommendations.” Nobody controls what an AI system recommends.
  • “Stuff the page with keywords and questions.” Thin, repetitive pages help no one.

A simple checklist

  1. AI search crawlers allowed in robots.txt and in your CDN or firewall settings.
  2. Site verified in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, with a sitemap submitted.
  3. Clear homepage and one page per service.
  4. Accurate Organization and Service structured data.
  5. Consistent profiles on relevant directories.
  6. Helpful content that answers real customer questions.
  7. Monthly measurement.
